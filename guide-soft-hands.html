Soft hands explained

A hand with an ace counted as 11 cannot bust on the next card. That one property changes every decision, and it is where casual players give away the most value.

What makes a hand soft

An ace counts as 11 whenever that does not bust the hand, and as 1 when it would. A hand currently counting an ace as 11 is soft. The name matters less than the property it describes: a soft hand cannot bust on the next card. Draw a 9 to your soft 16 and the ace quietly drops to 1, leaving hard 15. You risked nothing by drawing.

That single property splits blackjack strategy in two. Hard hands are a constant negotiation with bust risk. Soft hands remove that risk for one card, and correct strategy responds by drawing and doubling far more aggressively than feels natural.

The mistake nearly everyone makes

Soft 17 and soft 18 look like made hands, and standing on them is probably the most common error in casual play. Consider what standing on soft 17 actually claims: that 17 is a total worth defending. It is not. A 17 never wins against a dealer who finishes; the best it does is push another 17. The dealer is required to draw from 16 or less precisely because low totals lose, and your soft 17 is a 17 that comes with a free redraw. Under the rules on this site there is no upcard against which standing on soft 17 is correct. None.

Soft 18 is the subtler case, and it is why the hand Ace,7 is the most instructive two cards in the game.

Ace,7: one hand, three plays

Against a dealer 3 through 6, you double. The dealer is likely to bust, your hand cannot bust on the draw, and any 10 keeps you at 18 anyway. This is the moment to press money onto the table.

Against a 2, 7 or 8, you stand. Your 18 beats the 17s a dealer 7 tends to make and pushes the 18s from an 8, and against these cards the doubling math no longer clears the bar.

Against a 9, 10 or Ace, you hit. This is the play that feels wrong and is not. Strong upcards finish on 19 and 20 often enough that your 18 is a losing hand, and a hand that loses standing but cannot bust drawing should draw. Deal Ace,7 against a 9 in the analyzer and watch hitting beat standing by a clear margin.

Soft doubling in one sentence

The soft doubles all follow one idea: double soft hands against a weak dealer, and the weaker your soft total, the weaker the dealer card you need. Soft 13 and 14 double only against 5 and 6, soft 15 and 16 against 4 through 6, soft 17 and 18 against 3 through 6. You are not doubling because your hand is good. You are doubling because the dealer is in trouble and your hand cannot hurt itself.

When soft turns hard

One habit completes the picture: recount after every draw. Soft 16 that catches a 9 is hard 15, and hard 15 plays by hard rules. The most expensive soft-hand errors happen after the hand has quietly stopped being soft.
