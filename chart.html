How to memorize the chart

Nobody learns this grid cell by cell. Learn it in layers, and each layer is small.

Start with the four absolutes: always split aces and 8s, never split 10s or 5s, always stand on hard 17 or more, always hit hard 8 or less. Next, learn the stiff-hand rule that covers most of the hard section: with hard 12 through 16, stand when the dealer shows 2 through 6 and hit when the dealer shows 7 or higher, with one exception (hit 12 against a 2 or 3). Then add the doubles: 11 doubles against everything but an Ace, 10 doubles against 2 through 9, and 9 doubles against 3 through 6. That handles the vast majority of hands you will actually be dealt.

The soft totals and remaining pairs come last, and the analyzer is the fastest teacher there: deal yourself the hand, read the numbers, and the logic sticks better than memorization ever does.

Why the chart looks the way it does

Notice the cliff between the dealer's 6 and 7. To the left the chart is full of stands, doubles and splits; to the right it turns into hits. That line is the dealer bust table made visible. Against 2 through 6 the dealer must draw from a breakable total, so you avoid busting first and press bets while the risk is on the house. Against 7 and up the dealer usually finishes with a made hand, so you must improve your own total even at the risk of breaking.
