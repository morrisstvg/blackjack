Blackjack myths the math disagrees with

Common beliefs about blackjack, tested against the numbers.

Why blackjack breeds myths

Blackjack sits in an awkward psychological spot: it involves genuine decisions, so it feels like a game of reads and momentum, but the correct decisions are fixed and the outcomes are noise around a small negative average. Human pattern-matching fills that gap with stories. Every myth below is one of those stories, and every one of them dissolves against the same fact: the shoe has no memory and no awareness of who is betting what.

"The table is hot" and other streak beliefs

After four wins in a row, the fifth hand is dealt from the same shoe by the same rules with the same expectation as any other hand. Streaks are real in the sense that they happen constantly, and meaningless in the sense that they predict nothing. Believing a streak will continue and believing a losing table is "due" to turn are the same error pointing in opposite directions. The cards do not know what just happened.

The third-base player who "takes the dealer's bust card"

This is the myth with the strongest social life, since it comes with someone to blame. A bad player at third base hits when he should stand, draws a ten, and the dealer then makes a hand instead of busting; the table groans. What the table never counts is the equally frequent opposite case, where the same bad play consumes the card that would have completed the dealer's hand. An unknown card helps or hurts you with exactly balanced probability, so other players' decisions have zero effect on your long-run results. The numbers on this site are computed without any reference to other players at the table, because none is needed.

Betting systems

The Martingale, doubling your bet after every loss, is the most seductive because it genuinely wins most sessions: many small wins, punctuated by rare catastrophic losses when a losing run meets the table limit or the bottom of your wallet. Multiply it out and the expectation is unchanged, because no arrangement of bets alters the edge on each individual hand. Every progression system, positive or negative, redistributes when you lose without changing how much. If a betting pattern could beat a fixed negative edge, casinos would not offer the game.

Insurance as "protection"

Insurance is marketed by its name. It is a separate side bet that the dealer's hole card is a ten, and that bet needs to win one time in three to break even while it actually wins about 30.8% of the time. Whether you hold a blackjack, a 20 or a stiff changes nothing about that arithmetic. The even-money offer on your own blackjack is the identical bet in a friendlier costume, and declining it earns more over time.

"That dealer is unlucky for me"

Dealers shuffle, deal and follow forced rules. They have no more influence over the cards than the chair does. Switching tables to escape a dealer costs nothing and gains nothing.

Why this matters beyond trivia

Myths are not free. Chasing streaks inflates bets at random moments, blaming other players poisons the one social game the casino offers, and betting systems convert a slow, cheap edge into occasional ruin. Memorize the chart, pick tables with good rules, and every superstition becomes something you can happily ignore.
